## TL;DR

- **Z.AI and Moonshot AI** are falling behind on their next flagship models, Reuters reports, as a US compute rule and anti-distillation safeguards bite; **Nvidia** has reportedly paused server shipments to four Southeast Asian countries.
- **The FCC** authorized SpaceX's "Starmind" constellation of up to 1 million orbital data-center satellites on Nov 25, with the full network due by Nov 25, 2040.
- **Anthropic** says Claude agents helped migrate its Messages API from Python to Rust, cutting CPU per request by about 70% and median time to first token by about 20%.
- **OpenAI** will make ChatGPT and Codex share one usage allowance on Plus, Pro and Business plans from December, and is piloting "Private Inference" with a small group of business customers.
- **Japan** is drafting AI law changes that would allow on-site inspections of AI firms with weak safeguards, foreign developers included.
- **Canada** added $700 million to its AI Compute Access Fund on Nov 26, bringing it to $1 billion.

## Top stories

### 1. US compute rule and anti-distillation safeguards slow Chinese AI labs

**What happened.** Reuters reported on Nov 26 that Z.AI (Zhipu) planned to release its roughly 3-trillion-parameter GLM-5.5 this month but has not, and that a new US Commerce rule could push it into Q1 2027 or later, according to people familiar with the matter. Moonshot AI reportedly expects the gap between major releases to stretch from six months to a year or more if it loses overseas compute, with Kimi K4 possibly slipping to around mid-2027. The rule, in effect since Nov 19, requires licenses for compute arrangements at overseas data centers above a threshold roughly equal to 750 Nvidia H100s. Separately, the Wall Street Journal and Financial Times reported that Nvidia told partners including Dell and Super Micro to pause server shipments to Malaysia, Singapore, Thailand and Indonesia while it checks export compliance.

**Why it matters.** Renting Nvidia capacity abroad, plus distilling (training on outputs from stronger models), has been how Chinese labs kept costs low. Both routes are narrowing at once. The sources are anonymous and the companies did not comment to Reuters, so treat the timelines as estimates. If you rent GPUs in Southeast Asia, expect supply delays and more paperwork.

**Sources:** [Reuters via Investing.com](https://www.investing.com/news/stock-market-news/exclusiveus-chip-curbs-distillation-crackdown-threaten-chinese-ai-model-development-sources-say-5052154) · [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/26/nvidia-hits-pause-on-chip-shipments-to-southeast-asia/)

### 2. FCC authorizes SpaceX's 1-million-satellite "Starmind" data-center constellation

**What happened.** On Nov 25 the FCC granted SpaceX authority to deploy up to 1,000,000 "orbital compute modules", which it calls the first US authorization for a constellation of data centers in space. Aviation Week reports the grant requires the first orbital data center by Nov 25, 2033, 10% of the constellation by 2035, half by 2038 and all of it by Nov 25, 2040. Amazon's Kuiper, Viasat and the American Astronomical Society had petitioned to deny the application, CNBC reports.

**Why it matters.** This is a license to try, not proof it works. Critics question the engineering and costs, and an Earthjustice attorney told CNBC the FCC skipped the environmental review required by law; the FCC says environmental and astronomy concerns are outside its authority. For builders it is a long-dated signal: power, not chips alone, is now the limit that big players are trying to escape.

### 3. Anthropic used Claude agents to move its Messages API to Rust

**What happened.** In a Nov 25 engineering post, Anthropic said Claude wrote a first version of the API's core request path in about a week, but proving it matched the Python service took far longer. The Rust version passed about 98% of its tests in April, yet only about a quarter of inputs matched on production traffic. Anthropic then compared both versions on live requests and dispatched agents to fix differences. It says the Rust API uses about 70% less CPU per request and cuts median time to first token by about 20%, shipped without taking the service offline. API traffic grew roughly 1,800 times from early 2024 to fall 2026, the company says.

**Why it matters.** It is a candid case study of where AI coding is now: generating code is the easy part, verifying it is the hard part. The 98%-of-tests versus 25%-in-production gap is the key lesson for anyone migrating a live system with agents: test suites miss how real users behave, so build a way to compare old and new on real traffic first.

**Sources:** [Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/research/migrating-api-to-rust)

### 4. OpenAI merges ChatGPT and Codex limits, pilots Private Inference

**What happened.** OpenAI's Help Center says that starting in December, Plus, Pro and Business plans will share one usage allowance across ChatGPT and Codex, measured in tokens; Instant chat will not count. OpenAI says it expects most users won't hit resets more often than now, and users can wait, upgrade or buy credits. Separately, Sam Altman said on Nov 25 that OpenAI is testing Private Inference, an alpha feature that uses confidential computing to protect business prompts and replies during processing, with a small group of customers, The Next Web reports.

**Why it matters.** If you use Codex heavily, long coding runs will now draw down the same budget as your reasoning chats, so watch Settings → Usage once it changes. Private Inference targets companies that cannot send sensitive data to a model provider, but no pricing, model list or date for wider rollout has been published.

**Sources:** [OpenAI Help Center](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/20001625-understanding-chatgpt-and-codex-usage-limits) · [The Verge](https://www.theverge.com/ai-artificial-intelligence/1042187/openai-is-combining-chatgpt-and-codex-usage-limits) · [The Next Web](https://thenextweb.com/news/openai-private-inference-testing-customers)

### 5. Japan drafts tougher AI rules with inspections and penalties

**What happened.** Japan is preparing changes to its AI Promotion Act that would let authorities inspect AI companies with inadequate safety measures, and foreign developers such as OpenAI and Anthropic would be covered, Nikkei Asia reported. Citing the Yomiuri Shimbun, Seoul Economic Daily says measures under review include blocking answers about cyberattack methods and making harmful substances or bombs, and verifying users of AI with strong cyberattack capabilities. Firms ignoring a recommendation would get a corrective order, and penalties would follow if that order is violated; the specific penalties are still to be decided.

**Why it matters.** Japan's current law is mostly voluntary. This would add enforcement tied to a surge of AI-enabled cyberattacks. It is a draft, so details may change, but global model providers serving Japanese users should expect identity checks and incident reporting duties.

**Sources:** [Nikkei Asia](https://asia.nikkei.com/business/technology/artificial-intelligence/ai-firms-with-poor-safeguards-face-inspections-under-japan-draft-law) · [Seoul Economic Daily](https://en.sedaily.com/world/2026/11/26/japan-weighs-rules-making-ai-firms-liable-for-unsafe-answers)

### 6. Canada tops up its AI Compute Access Fund to $1 billion

**What happened.** On Nov 26 in Toronto, AI Minister Evan Solomon announced a $700 million next phase of the AI Compute Access Fund, building on the initial $300 million for a $1 billion total. The fund offsets compute costs for Canadian companies. The new phase uses sector-focused calls, starting with health and life sciences, expands incentives for sovereign (Canadian) compute capacity, and names funding for 45 more companies.

**Why it matters.** Governments are now subsidizing compute directly and steering it toward domestic providers. For Canadian startups it is cheaper GPU time; for everyone else it is another sign that "sovereign compute" is becoming industrial policy.

**Sources:** [Innovation, Science and Economic Development Canada](https://www.canada.ca/en/innovation-science-economic-development/news/2026/11/government-of-canada-boosts-the-ai-compute-access-fund-with-700-million-expansion.html)

## Deep dive: Compute access is the new front line

Three of today's stories (US curbs on overseas compute, a million-satellite license and Canada's subsidy) are about the same thing: who gets to use large amounts of AI compute, and where it sits.

**Context.** Export controls used to focus on shipping chips. Chinese labs adapted by renting Nvidia capacity in data centers abroad. The Commerce rule that took effect Nov 19 targets that route directly: according to Reuters, compute arrangements above roughly 750 H100-equivalents at overseas data centers now need licenses, existing operators have 90 days to get them, and chip shipments to Malaysia, Singapore, Indonesia, Thailand and Brazil need licenses too, with exceptions for qualifying US companies.

**What changes.**

1. *Release cycles for Chinese frontier models may slow.* Moonshot reportedly expects its six-month cadence could double, and Z.AI's GLM-5.5 may slip into 2027. Moonshot is reportedly seeking European capacity and Chinese chips instead.
2. *Distillation is getting harder.* Reuters reports that safeguards from OpenAI and Anthropic have already slowed both labs, and that Moonshot has started distilling from Google and Meta models instead.
3. *Regional GPU supply tightens.* Nvidia's reported pause in Southeast Asia affects everyone renting there, not only Chinese customers.
4. *Rich countries compete on where compute lives.* Canada pays firms to use domestic compute; the US FCC is licensing compute in orbit.

**The catch.** Much of this rests on anonymous sourcing and plans that may not happen. Z.AI's release of GLM-5.4 this week shows labs can still ship improved models on existing compute, and Z.AI says the new design matches the performance of far larger models. Starmind's first data center isn't due until 2033.

**What to do now.** (a) If your product depends on a Chinese open-weight model, don't assume a fast upgrade cadence; plan on today's versions for longer. (b) If you rent GPUs in Southeast Asia, ask your provider how it is handling the new license rules and the 90-day window. (c) If you're a Canadian company, check whether the health and life sciences call fits you.
