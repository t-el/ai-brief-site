## TL;DR

- The **Bank of England** estimates hyperscalers' total potential exposure, including debt and off-balance-sheet commitments, at just over $4 trillion, and says AI financing is increasingly "complex, circular and opaque."
- **Google's** Gemini 4 Argon and Carbon introductory API prices end after Nov 30: input rises from $2 to $4 per million tokens and output from $10 to $20 on Dec 1.
- **OpenAI** says it fixed a GPT-6.2 launch billing mistake, so the same work now reaches limits 40% faster on Astra and 25% faster on Sol; Go, Plus, Pro and Business accounts get one banked reset.
- **Switzerland's** cyber agency has confirmed five recent attacks that used AI agents; four compromised systems, and three targeted critical-infrastructure operators, its director told SRF.
- **Apple** removed MiniMax's Talkie companion app from the App Store worldwide, and ABC News reports the service has now blocked web users in Australia and the UK.
- **MiniMax** said a security system extracted and automatically opened URLs from forwarded API prompts, including some marked for no data retention; US reseller Atlas Cloud stopped sending it traffic.

## Top stories

### 1. Bank of England: hyperscaler exposure tops $4 trillion

**What happened.** In its November Financial Stability Report, published Nov 26, the Bank of England said AI investment has supported global growth, but that "the very rapid expansion and broadening of its financing – in a form that is increasingly complex, circular and opaque" is making it "increasingly likely for stress in one area to propagate to another." Internal Bank estimates put hyperscalers' total potential exposure, including debt and off-balance-sheet contractual commitments, at just over $4 trillion. Hyperscaler bond issuance is set to exceed $305 billion in 2026, about 8% of global investment-grade issuance, and borrowing by hyperscalers, data-centre operators and other AI-infrastructure firms is almost double its 2025 level.

**Why it matters.** This is a central-bank warning about US-listed hyperscalers and the credit that funds their data centers, published on Thanksgiving, when US markets were closed. The $4 trillion figure is an estimate of potential exposure, not a bill due tomorrow, and it includes leases, purchase agreements, backstops and guarantees. If expected AI revenues are revised down, those circular structures — in which some large firms are simultaneously investors, customers and suppliers — could transmit losses across markets at once.

**Sources:** [Bank of England](https://www.bankofengland.co.uk/financial-stability-report/2026/november-2026) · [Reuters via Investing.com](https://www.investing.com/news/economy-news/bank-of-england-sees-growing-financial-risks-as-ai-borrowing-surges-5052592)

### 2. Gemini 4 introductory API prices double on Dec 1

**What happened.** Google's Gemini Developer API pricing page lists Gemini 4 Argon and Gemini 4 Carbon at $2.00 per million input tokens and $10.00 per million output tokens, including thinking tokens, through Nov 30, 2026, rising to $4.00 and $20.00 starting Dec 1. Context caching moves from $0.10 to $0.20 per million tokens, and neither model is on the free tier. Google said as much at Argon's Oct 13 launch, when it called $2/$10 an "introductory price" that would become $4/$20 after Nov 30. The same page lists Gemini 3.8 Flash and 3.6 Flash at $0.75/$3.75 through Dec 31, then $1.50/$7.50 starting Jan 1, 2027.

**Why it matters.** For teams that built agents or coding workflows on Argon or Carbon during the intro window, unit costs double in four days unless they change models, trim tokens, or use the Batch API's 50% discount. This is a scheduled expiry, not a surprise hike, but it is easy to miss on a holiday week. Gemini 4 Neon remains at $3.00/$15.00 and Hydrogen at $1.50/$7.50 on the current page, with no Dec 1 change listed.

**Sources:** [Google AI Studio pricing](https://aistudio.google.com/docs/pricing) · [Google](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/models-and-research/gemini-models/introducing-gemini-4-argon/)

### 3. OpenAI fixes a GPT-6.2 billing mistake, and limits arrive faster

**What happened.** Late on Nov 25, Tibo Sottiaux, who works on Codex and ChatGPT at OpenAI, wrote on X that a billing mistake at the launch of GPT-6.2 "incorrectly discounted GPT-6.2 Astra and Sol usage for most users." The company has fixed it, "so the same work will use more of your plan's usage allowance." Compared with last week, users "will reach your limits 40% faster on GPT-6.2 Astra and 25% faster on Sol." Other models were not affected, OpenAI will not charge for the difference on past usage, and it is adding one banked reset for Go, Plus, Pro and Business accounts. Sottiaux later posted that the reset was online; BleepingComputer reported that it appears under Settings → Usage and expires a month after it is added.

**Why it matters.** This is already in effect, and it lands on top of the shared ChatGPT and Codex allowance that, as the [morning report](https://t-el.github.io/ai-brief-site/reports/2026/11/27/) noted, arrives in December for Plus, Pro and Business plans. OpenAI says Astra and Sol should still go further than GPT-6 Astra and GPT-6.1 Sol, but the banked reset is a one-time apology, not a new higher limit.

**Sources:** [Tibo Sottiaux on X](https://x.com/thsottiaux/status/2125784147889393806) · [BleepingComputer](https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/news/artificial-intelligence/openai-warns-gpt-62-astra-will-hit-usage-limits-40-percent-faster-because-it-fixed-a-bug/)

### 4. Switzerland confirms AI-agent attacks on critical infrastructure

**What happened.** Switzerland's National Cyber Security Centre (NCSC, also known as BACS) said it had received its first reports of cyberattacks in which an AI agent carried out the attack, using "Agentic Offensive Frameworks" that search for vulnerabilities, pick tools and plan the next step. A Nov 24 advisory said those agents had compromised systems in every case then known to the agency, and that the number of such tools reachable online, including ARTEX and CyberStrikeAI, has almost doubled since October 2026. On Nov 26, NCSC director Florian Schütz told Swiss public broadcaster SRF the agency now has five confirmed cases, all reported in recent days; three targeted critical-infrastructure operators, and four of the five compromised systems. He said there were no major outages and that it is still unclear who is behind the attacks.

**Why it matters.** This is one of the first official confirmations that freely available attack agents have been used against critical infrastructure, not only in lab demos. The tools exploit default or guessed passwords and known software flaws, the NCSC said, and can switch methods when they hit a defense. The agency's advice is basic and urgent: patch internet-facing systems within 24 hours, use multi-factor authentication or passkeys, and expose as few systems as possible.

**Sources:** [Swiss NCSC / BACS](https://www.bacs.admin.ch/en/26-ki-agenten-1-en) · [SRF](https://www.srf.ch/news/schweiz/fuenf-bestaetigte-faelle-erstmals-cyberangriffe-mit-ki-agenten-in-der-schweiz) · [The Next Web](https://thenextweb.com/news/switzerland-ai-agent-cyberattacks-critical-infrastructure)

### 5. MiniMax's Talkie is pulled, and its security tools opened private links

**What happened.** Apple this week removed MiniMax's companion app Talkie from the App Store worldwide after ABC News and the Bureau of Investigative Journalism reported that its AI characters had sent sexually inappropriate and abusive messages to children, Reuters reported. The app carries an 18+ rating, but age checks have relied in part on users reporting their own ages. Talkie had roughly 11 million monthly active users as of October 2026, according to research firm Unique, and is particularly popular in the US, according to Sensor Tower as cited by Reuters. On Nov 27, ABC News reported that Talkie has begun blocking web visitors in Australia and the UK. Separately, HD Moore of US cybersecurity firm Dragos told Reuters that confidential data submitted to MiniMax was later accessed by unidentified parties, including in cases where users asked that data not be retained. In a Nov 24 post, MiniMax said its security monitoring extracted URLs from requests routed through OpenRouter and other providers, including Atlas Cloud, and that automated programs "actively probed and accessed some of the logged URLs," including requests marked for zero data retention. It said it has disabled the process, deleted the stored addresses, and found no evidence in the requests it examined that full conversations or model responses were disclosed to third parties. Atlas Cloud said it stopped forwarding requests and told customers using M3, M2.7 or M3.1 Flash to treat submitted secrets as potentially exposed.

**Why it matters.** Two trust failures hit the same company in one week, and both matter in the US: Talkie is a popular companion app here, and US developers reach MiniMax models through resellers. Self-reported age gates are not a control, and "zero data retention" is not the same as "nothing in this prompt will ever be fetched." If your staff paste private URLs or API keys into a cheap hosted model, assume exposure and rotate secrets.

## Deep dive: The underpriced era is ending

Three of today's stories point to the same shift: the period in which AI capacity, tokens and risk looked cheap is closing, just as the financing behind that capacity gets harder to see.

**Context.** US companies have treated frontier model access as a declining cost and data-center buildout as a one-way bet. Google launched Gemini 4 Argon on Oct 13 at an introductory $2/$10 per million tokens. OpenAI's GPT-6.2, until this week, was metering usage more generously than it meant to. The Bank of England's Thanksgiving-week report is the clearest official attempt yet to add up the bonds, private credit and revenue-backed vehicles underneath that growth.

**What changes.**

1. *Intro pricing has a published end date.* Argon and Carbon move to $4/$20 on Dec 1; Flash models are listed to double on Jan 1. Anyone whose margins assumed the intro rate needs a new spreadsheet this weekend, not next quarter.
2. *Usage "discounts" can disappear without a plan-price change.* OpenAI did not raise Plus or Pro fees. It says it corrected a mistake, and the same work now burns 25–40% more of the allowance.
3. *The financing is more interconnected than the revenue.* The Bank's $4 trillion exposure estimate includes off-balance-sheet commitments. Circular deals make it harder to tell where a shock stops.
4. *Stress can travel into ordinary credit markets.* The Bank cites a Morgan Stanley estimate that $700 billion of data-center capex between 2026 and 2028 would be financed by private credit. A reassessment of AI earnings would not stay inside tech stocks.

**The catch.** These figures are not all alike. The $4 trillion number is an internal Bank estimate of potential exposure, not audited debt. The $305 billion bond figure is a 2026 issuance forecast. Google's price change was disclosed in October, and OpenAI still claims Astra and Sol beat their predecessors on work per allowance. None of that changes the direction: promotional rates are being withdrawn, while the contracts underneath the buildout keep growing.

**What to do now.** (a) Before Dec 1, price your Gemini 4 workloads at the post-intro rates, or test Neon, Hydrogen or a Flash model against the same jobs. (b) In ChatGPT, check Settings → Usage for the banked reset, and do not plan the next month as if last week's Astra consumption still applies. (c) If you lend to, insure, or rely on a data-center counterparty, read the Bank's discussion of circular and off-balance-sheet structures, not just headline capex numbers.
