## Top stories

### 1. OpenAI countersues Apple, alleging trade-secret theft

**What happened.** On Friday, Nov 27, OpenAI filed counterclaims against Apple in California federal court, Reuters reports. OpenAI says Apple rehired a former employee who spent six months at OpenAI and had access to plans for a new device and its future hardware roadmap. OpenAI also says Apple discouraged suppliers from working on OpenAI devices and that 400 employees have moved from Apple to OpenAI so far. Apple denies the claims and called the counterclaims "a transparent attempt to distract from the substance of Apple's lawsuit," which it filed in July against OpenAI and two former Apple staff.

**Why it matters.** The case shows how much OpenAI is betting on consumer hardware, and that it now treats Apple as a direct rival. For hardware suppliers, the dispute raises the cost of working with both companies at once. All of these are allegations in a court filing; none has been tested yet.

**Sources:** [Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/5f374b32b07b/technology/openai-countersues-apple-stealing-trade-secrets-ft-reports-2026-11-27/)

### 2. Sber releases GigaChat 4 as open weights; Putin proposes AI strategy to 2036

**What happened.** At the AI Journey conference in Moscow on Nov 26, Sber released GigaChat 4, a mixture-of-experts (MoE) model with 708B total and 41B active parameters per token, a one-million-token context window and instant and reasoning modes in one checkpoint. The weights are on Hugging Face under the MIT license. Sber also released a smaller 37B model; Vedomosti reports individuals can use that one free for non-commercial purposes. In its technical report, Sber says GigaChat 4 scores 71.26% on Terminal-Bench 2.0 in reasoning mode, up from 30.30% for GigaChat 3.5, and performs comparably to DeepSeek V4 Flash and Mistral 4 Large on selected coding and agent benchmarks. At the same event, President Vladimir Putin proposed extending Russia's national AI strategy through 2036 and adopting a national action plan, TASS reports.

**Why it matters.** MIT is about as permissive as model licenses get, so the 708B model can be used commercially without revenue clauses. But it is large: Sber's own instructions run the FP8 version on two nodes of eight H100 80GB GPUs each. All benchmark figures are Sber's own, under its evaluation setup. Putin framed the work as building "sovereign foundation models," so expect state priorities to shape the roadmap.

**Sources:** [Hugging Face (Sber model card)](https://huggingface.co/ai-sage/GigaChat4-708B-A41B) · [GigaChat blog](https://giga.chat/blog/en/posts/gigachat-4-tech-report) · [Vedomosti](https://www.vedomosti.ru/technology/news/2026/11/26/1415693-novie-modeli) · [TASS](https://tass.com/politics/2225503)

### 3. Banned Nvidia Blackwell racks turn up at a Megaspeed-linked Shanghai site

**What happened.** Bloomberg reported on Nov 27 that two Chinese companies installed Nvidia Blackwell hardware at the Yangtze River Delta Project, a Shanghai data center with ties to Alice Huang and Megaspeed. Loan filings show Shanghai Shuoyao pledged a GB300 NVL72 rack as collateral for a 96 million yuan ($14 million) loan, and Shanghai Kezhan listed a GB200 server at the site. Bloomberg traced the rack's serial number through a public warranty database to Taiwan's Gigabyte, which declined to say who first bought it. Exporting these chips to China without a US license is banned. Bloomberg says it has no evidence that Megaspeed itself diverted hardware to the site, and Megaspeed denies wrongdoing.

**Why it matters.** This is a new development after the US added Megaspeed to its Entity List this month. It shows restricted hardware reaching China despite the rules, and Chinese lenders, including ones with government ties, accepting it as loan collateral. Expect more pressure on server makers and distributors to track where their racks end up, which could mean more paperwork for any company buying GPU servers through third countries.

**Sources:** [The Business Times (Bloomberg)](https://www.businesstimes.com.sg/companies-markets/nvidia-blackwell-chips-turn-china-site-tied-alice-huang) · [The Decoder](https://the-decoder.com/banned-nvidia-blackwell-chips-turn-up-in-a-shanghai-data-center-linked-to-alice-huang-bloomberg-reports/)

### 4. Tencent open-sources DFly2 to speed up long-context inference

**What happened.** On Nov 26, Tencent's Hunyuan AI Infra team released DFly2 in full: weights, training code (in its AngelSpec framework) and a technical report. DFly2 is a drafter for speculative decoding, where a small model guesses several tokens and the large model checks them in one pass. Instead of keeping its own memory of the prompt, DFly2 reuses the large model's KV cache (its stored record of earlier tokens) and the positions its sparse attention already selected. The authors report 62% and 81% more throughput than the stronger baseline on long-context and agentic sets at 64K tokens when serving DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash.

**Why it matters.** Serving cost and speed for long agent runs depend heavily on tricks like this. The catch: DFly2 only works with models that already use sparse attention (drafters are released for Hy4 flash, DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash and GLM-5.3-Flash), the gains are the authors' own measurements, and AngelSpec ships under its own "AngelSpec License," so check terms before production use.

**Sources:** [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.34776) · [GitHub (Tencent/AngelSpec)](https://github.com/Tencent/AngelSpec)

### 5. Poland signs $300 million AI software deal with Palantir

**What happened.** Poland signed an agreement on Nov 27 with Palantir worth about $300 million over five years for software that uses AI to support army commanders' decisions, Reuters reports. Defence Minister Wladyslaw Kosiniak-Kamysz called it "a watershed moment" and said "all keys, servers, and information" will remain the property of the Polish Armed Forces. Poland signed a memorandum of understanding with Palantir in 2025.

**Why it matters.** The minister's emphasis on data ownership reflects European concern, noted by Reuters, about relying on US defence technology that could be cut off. Vendors selling AI to governments should expect data-control terms like these to become standard asks.

**Sources:** [Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/5f374b32b07b/technology/poland-signs-contract-with-palantir-worth-300-mln-over-5-years-2026-11-27/)

### 6. OpenAI fixes GPT-6.2 billing error; limits now run out faster

**What happened.** OpenAI's Thibault Sottiaux said on Nov 26 that a billing mistake at the Nov 17 launch of GPT-6.2 Astra and Sol had wrongly discounted usage for most users, The Next Web reports. After the fix, the same work uses up plan allowances 40% faster on Astra and 25% faster on Sol than last week. OpenAI says it won't charge for past usage and is adding one banked reset for everyone on Go, Plus, Pro and Business. This is separate from the combined usage limits covered yesterday.

**Why it matters.** If you planned workloads around last week's limits, those plans are now off. A banked reset is a one-time refresh you choose when to use; OpenAI's help center says it restores your main allowances but does not raise limits or add credits.

**Sources:** [The Next Web](https://thenextweb.com/news/openai-billing-mistake-gpt-6-2-usage-limits) · [OpenAI Help Center](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/20001498-how-resets-work-in-codex)

## Deep dive: Open weights keep coming from outside the US

**Context.** Two of this week's most practical releases for builders came from Russia and China, not the US. Sber's GigaChat 4 is a 708B MoE model under the MIT license. Tencent's DFly2 is serving infrastructure that makes sparse-attention models, many of them Chinese open-weight releases, cheaper to run on long contexts. Both arrive as the US tightens control over who can use advanced chips, and as Bloomberg shows those controls still leak.

**What changes.**

1. *The license bar keeps dropping.* MIT on a 708B model means no revenue thresholds and no user caps. That puts pressure on labs that release weights under custom licenses with commercial limits.
2. *Efficiency work is shipping in the open.* Both releases treat serving cost as part of the product. GigaChat 4 supports two speculative-decoding drafters, and DFly2's whole point is to avoid extra memory and prefill cost. GigaChat 4's model card lists a DFlash2 drafter, and DFly2 is benchmarked against DFlash-style drafters.
3. *Sparse attention is becoming the default for long context.* GigaChat 4 uses DeepSeek Sparse Attention. DFly2 only works on sparse models. Tooling is starting to assume this design.
4. *Sovereignty is a stated goal.* Putin explicitly backed "sovereign foundation models" and warned about "shadow" use of foreign AI at work. Expect GigaChat's roadmap to follow national priorities.

**The catch.** Every benchmark number here is self-reported under the developer's own setup. A 708B model needs serious hardware: Sber documents a 16-GPU H100 deployment for the FP8 weights. The 37B GigaChat model is not under the same terms as the flagship; Vedomosti reports it is free only for non-commercial use by individuals. DFly2 ships under a custom license. And for some companies, compliance, sanctions or procurement rules may limit the use of models from Russian or Chinese developers regardless of license.

**What to do now.** (a) If you self-host large models, add GigaChat 4 to your evaluation list, but run your own tests before trusting the benchmark tables. (b) If you serve DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash, GLM-5.3-Flash or Hy4 flash at long context, try the released DFly2 drafters and measure throughput on your own traffic. (c) Before shipping any of these, have legal review the license and any sanctions questions; MIT on the model card doesn't settle the second.
